Harmony

The scale and the chords: a preset sets the scale, the progression and how the chords are made; not the key.

Scale

The notes everything is made of. A preset fills the wheel; then add or remove pitches on it. The key is above the wheel.

Progression

How the chords move: freely, in cycles that come home, or round a set loop. Chords are named by the scale degree they stand on (I is the home chord), so a progression fits any key and scale.

In the scale

How to read the wheel The home note is in the middle; the twelve tones are around the rim, clockwise from the top. Each bead is a pitch, at its exact place around the circle, in its colour: the colour follows the place, from amber at the home note round the colour wheel. Just ratios lie inside, nearer the middle (and bigger) the simpler the ratio: the fifth, 3/2, is nearest. Equal-tempered pitches lie on the dashed outer ring. Filled: in the scale. Tap a bead to add or remove that pitch; tap a tone's name to add or remove the tone. With more than one pitch for a tone, each chord uses the one purest against its root. While it plays: white halos and a dashed white shape mark the chord (the thickest halo is its root); a diamond marks each melody note, with a dotted line through the last few; a square, each bass line note; a triangle, each arpeggio note; a star, each glint. Around the outside, the drum band is the bar: 16 steps clockwise from the top, with lanes for kick, snare and clap, hats and rim, and a white playhead.
